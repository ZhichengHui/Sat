## Rust (doc comments only)

- `Search`: the invariant of the heap. It is ordered by `before` (activity descending, then index
  ascending), and no entry comes before its parent. `hpos[v] = NONE` outside the heap.
  `heap[hlen..]` has no meaning. `sok` says nothing about the heap, since its vectors keep length
  `n`. `rescale` rebuilds the heap: the shift keeps `≥` but can make activities equal, and then
  the index can put a parent after its child. The comment also says why `sift_up`, `heapify`,
  `reinsert` and `pick_heap` are `#[inline(never)]`: +1.4 % instructions in `cdcl` on hole9
  without it, see #143.
- `HEAP_MIN`: why 512. It lies between the small guard instances (≤ 100 variables, +3 to +5 %
  with the heap) and bmc (≥ 13215 variables, where the scan took 24–58 % of the instructions).
  hanoi4 (718 variables) is neutral. The bound changes only speed, not decisions.
- `rescale`: its doc now says it rebuilds the heap.
- The regenerated `urust/` is byte-identical to main, because the front end drops doc comments.
  PLACEHOLDERS 0.
- **Rust simplification not made.** In `insert`, `self.use_heap &&` is redundant: its only caller
  `reinsert` is called only under `if self.use_heap`. Removing it keeps the decisions, but it
  changes the generated code of an `#[inline(never)]` function, and #143 showed that such changes
  move instruction counts. It needs a benchmark, not a review, so I left it out.
